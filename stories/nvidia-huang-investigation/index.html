Jensen Huang resigns: how the Nvidia espionage reports unfolded

In two weeks, a report that Nvidia’s founder was targeted by Chinese intelligence led to a board inquiry, another allegation, a share slide and his resignation.

In 30 seconds

For everyone

What happened

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO on 12 November, two weeks after The New York Times reported that US intelligence found he had a close relationship with a Megaspeed executive they concluded was working for Beijing. Huang admitted infidelity, the board hired outside investigators, and a second alleged relationship, with a senior Nvidia executive, was reported on 11 November.

Why it matters

Nvidia makes the chips behind most of the world’s AI and is one of the most valuable companies on earth. Its founder leaving under investigation raises questions about US–China chip security, who runs the company next, and what it means for investors.

What’s next

Interim CEO Colette Kress leads Nvidia's earnings on 17 November. Huang has agreed to appear voluntarily before House committees, and the board is searching for a permanent CEO.

    New York Times report: Huang targeted by Chinese intelligence

    US intelligence agencies found Huang had developed a close relationship with an executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, one of Nvidia’s biggest Southeast Asian customers, whom US officials concluded was gathering information for Beijing. Officials did not believe Huang knew. Nvidia said the interactions were a normal part of his job. China called the claims baseless smears.
