All US flights halted: the FAA ground stop, hour by hour

Why every departure in the United States stopped for about four hours on Friday, what officials say caused it, and what it means if you are flying this weekend.

In 30 seconds

For everyone

What happened

On Friday afternoon US time (early Saturday in India), the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), which runs US air traffic control, stopped every flight from taking off at US airports after a communications failure hit several of its air traffic control centres. The stop was lifted about four hours later.

Why it matters

It was only the second nationwide ground stop since the 11 September 2001 attacks, and thousands of flights were cancelled. Planes and crews ended up in the wrong places, so delays and cancellations are expected through the weekend.

What’s next

Officials say there was no cyber attack. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blames old equipment and says more outages will follow unless it is replaced. Watch for a fuller explanation and for how quickly airlines recover.

    FAA stops all departures across the US

    Just before 4 pm US Eastern time on Friday, the FAA orders a ground stop for all US airports after "communication issues impacting multiple Air Route Traffic Control Centers", which guide planes at high altitude between airports. Planes already in the air keep flying to their destinations. Some flights over the ocean and from Alaska and Hawaii are allowed to leave.

    Duffy blames old equipment: "It got overloaded"

    At a late-night press conference, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says old switches and routers that carry air traffic data were overloaded and began giving inconsistent data. Mullin says new security patches added to the older system appear to have overwhelmed the routers, and that the outage is not linked to the Microsoft outage a day earlier. Duffy warns there will be more such outages unless the system is upgraded and calls for funding, saying "This is an emergency."

What this means for you

Flying to, from or within the US this weekend? Check your flight status with the airline before leaving for the airport. Delta and United are letting affected passengers change flights without a fee. Under US Transportation Department rules, if your flight is cancelled and you do not accept another flight, you are entitled to a refund.
