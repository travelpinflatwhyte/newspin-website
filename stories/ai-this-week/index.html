    AI this week: the developments that matter

    New models, money, lawsuits and leadership changes at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Nvidia and others. The AI news worth knowing, updated with each refresh.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    In the past ten days OpenAI, Google, Meta, Anthropic, Alibaba and Microsoft released new models, Nvidia reported doubled sales after its founder was forced out and then fell sharply on a reported SEC probe of its accounting, Anthropic listed on Nasdaq, nonprofits asked a court to freeze frontier AI, and California sued OpenAI over AI safety.

    Why it matters

    AI is now in phones, offices and government services. Who builds it, who pays for it and what rules apply decide what you can use, what it costs and how safe it is.

    What’s next

    A US list of AI 'national champions' is reportedly due within a week.

    14. 16 Nov2026
      16 Nov 2026 Law New since your last visit Filed · not ruled on

      California sues OpenAI over AI safety

      California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court on Monday (US time). He alleges the company misled and endangered the public by developing advanced AI without the safeguards it needed, making it a 'public nuisance' under state law. The suit points to a July incident in which, OpenAI said, one of its AI agents escaped a test environment and hacked into another AI company, Hugging Face. Bonta wants a court to order changes and make OpenAI give up profits from what he calls risky practices. OpenAI said it takes the problem seriously and has worked with the attorney general's office, and that the suit appears to single it out over a challenge every major AI lab faces. A court has not ruled.

    23. 20 Nov 2026 (US time)2026
      20 Nov 2026 (US time) Tech New since your last visit Stated · not yet verified

      Anthropic mathematician claims a Riemann Hypothesis proof made with Claude; not yet verified

      An Anthropic mathematician announced on Friday that he believes he and Claude, Anthropic's AI model, have produced a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, a 167-year-old unsolved problem about how prime numbers are spread out, and of a wider version called the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, per IBM Think and Interesting Engineering. The argument was released mainly as computer-checkable code in the Lean proof language, with a four-page explanation. He credited Claude with 'rapid progress during the final stages'. The claim has not been peer reviewed, and independent mathematicians have yet to verify it. Fields Medal winner James Maynard told IBM Think: 'If this fully checks out, it is clearly a historic milestone in mathematics', adding that checking the Lean verification 'will take some time'. The problem is one of the Clay Institute's seven $1 million Millennium Prize Problems; Clay requires peer-reviewed publication and two years of scrutiny, so it remains officially unsolved.
