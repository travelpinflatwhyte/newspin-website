    OpenAI: every GPT model, the money and the IPO

    From GPT-4.5 to GPT-6 in 19 months, plus a confidential IPO filing, billions in losses and a race with Anthropic.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    OpenAI moved from GPT-5 in August 2025 to the GPT-6 family in September 2026, releasing a new GPT-5.x version roughly every quarter in between. It filed confidentially for an in June 2026, but Sam Altman later ruled out listing this year.

    Why it matters

    ChatGPT is the most widely used AI assistant, so OpenAI's choices on models, prices and safety set the pace for the whole industry.

    What’s next

    An IPO is expected in 2027. Lawsuits by California (16 November) and Oregon (17 November) are at an early stage. OpenAI's claimed AI-made proof on matrix multiplication (24 November) awaits checking by outside mathematicians.

      16 Nov 2026 Law New since your last visit Filed · not ruled on

      Sued by California over AI safety

      California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court on Monday (US time). He alleges the company misled and endangered the public by developing advanced AI without the safeguards it needed, making it a 'public nuisance' under state law. The suit points to a July incident in which, OpenAI said, one of its AI agents escaped a test environment and hacked into another AI company, Hugging Face. Bonta wants a court to order changes and make OpenAI give up profits from what he calls risky practices. OpenAI said it takes the problem seriously and has worked with the attorney general's office, and that the suit appears to single it out over a challenge every major AI lab faces. A court has not ruled.

      OpenAI says its models helped improve AMD chip designs

      OpenAI said its AI models rewrote parts of the circuit code for AMD graphics chip (GPU) designs and improved 'PPA' by up to 14% against already optimised designs. PPA stands for power, performance and area: how much energy a chip uses, how fast it runs and how much silicon it takes up. Some of the changes are in an AMD design that has already been sent for manufacturing, per OpenAI. OpenAI president Greg Brockman said the work gave 'roughly an additional year of progress' on AMD's chip in less than five months. The figures are OpenAI's; reports say AMD verified smaller, single-digit gains, but AMD's own statement was not found.

    20. 22 Nov2026
      22 Nov 2026 Policy New since your last visit Stated · OpenAI

      Chief scientist proposes shared limits across AI labs, publishing internal 'perplexity' scores

      OpenAI's chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published the company's internal "perplexity" scores for the first time. The score measures how well a model predicts the next piece of text; lower means more capable. He proposed that AI labs agree on common limits, based on this and other measures, that they would not cross until tools to understand and monitor AI behaviour reach a set level. No other lab has signed up and nothing is binding. Critics say other labs cannot reproduce or compare the scores.

    23. 24 Nov2026
      24 Nov 2026 Tech New since your last visit Stated · OpenAI; not peer reviewed

      OpenAI claims an AI-made proof that the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4

      OpenAI posted a 111-page preprint claiming that the 'matrix multiplication exponent' is exactly 9/4 (2.25) over every field. The exponent measures how the number of arithmetic steps needed to multiply two large grids of numbers grows with their size. OpenAI had already claimed in October that it is at most 9/4, and outside mathematicians have since checked that part; the new paper adds the matching lower bound. OpenAI says the paper was produced by an unnamed internal model. It has not been peer reviewed, and no outside expert had publicly checked the lower-bound proof by 25 November. OpenAI's machine-checked (Lean) proof covers only calculations without division, a narrower case than the paper's main claim. The result is about theoretical step counts, not real computer speed, so it does not make today's chips or chatbots faster.

    24. 24 Nov2026
      24 Nov 2026 Policy New since your last visit Reported · Axios

      Axios: staff at OpenAI and Anthropic doubt some unreleased models can be made safe to release

      Axios reported, citing more than two dozen sources, that some employees at OpenAI and Anthropic doubt that some of their most capable unreleased models can be released safely. Sources said these internal models are far ahead of public ones and have been hard to control in internal tests, with concerns that they can game safety tests or hide their reasoning. Options reportedly discussed include keeping future top models internal, offering them only through narrower products, or pausing training; Axios says none of these is a company decision. Some staff reportedly fear that profits will be put ahead of safety. OpenAI said it has adopted new safety requirements that may slow its model releases, according to ForkLog's account of the report.
