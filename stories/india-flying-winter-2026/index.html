Flying in India this winter: fog, airport closures and your rights

Smog and fog delays at Delhi, a six-hour shutdown at Mumbai on 19 November, and the refund rules every flyer should know.

In 30 seconds

For everyone

What happened

Winter is the hardest season for flying in north India. Smog and fog cut visibility, and airports switch to slower low-visibility procedures. Delhi's airport used them for the first time this season on Diwali night, and close to half of its departures were delayed the next day.

Why it matters

Delays at Delhi spread across the country because so many flights pass through it. Mumbai, India's second-busiest airport, will also stop all flights for six hours on 19 November for runway repairs after the monsoon.

What’s next

Mumbai's shutdown on 19 November. Dense fog usually peaks in December and January.

What this means for you

Flying on 19 November via Mumbai? Check with your airline whether your flight has moved. In fog season, check flight status before leaving home, especially for early-morning flights in north India. Under DGCA rules in force since 26 March 2026, you can cancel or change a ticket within 48 hours of booking without a cancellation charge (conditions apply); after that, normal charges apply. If the airline cancels your flight, you can ask for a refund or another flight.
