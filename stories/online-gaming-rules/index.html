    India’s online gaming crackdown and its new regulator

    Real-money games are banned. A new authority now decides what counts as esports or a social game.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Since 1 May 2026, online games with money stakes are banned, and the Online Gaming Authority of India classifies and registers games.

    Why it matters

    It reshaped one of India’s fastest-growing digital industries, from fantasy sports to esports and game studios.

    What’s next

    The Supreme Court is due to hear the challenge to the ban on 2 December; the ban stays in force meanwhile. The authority is also deciding which titles count as esports or social games.

    4. 19 Nov2026

      Supreme Court to hear the challenge to the ban on 2 December

      The company behind the A23 Rummy app, the lead petitioner challenging the 2025 gaming law, asked the Supreme Court for an earlier hearing, and a bench led by Chief Justice Surya Kant listed the case for 2 December, gaming trade outlets report. The petitioners argue the law wrongly treats games of skill like games of chance, breaks the right to do business, and covers betting, which the Constitution leaves to states. The case was moved to the Supreme Court from three High Courts in September 2025 and has been deferred before. A hearing date is not a stay: the ban stays in force unless the court rules otherwise.

    What this means for you

    Games with no money at stake can keep running without mandatory registration unless the authority asks. Platforms must store Indian users’ data in India and offer age checks and parental controls.
