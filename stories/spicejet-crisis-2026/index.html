    SpiceJet's troubles: cancellations, unpaid dues and a search for money

    How one of India's oldest budget airlines came to cancel one in five flights, what it owes, and how it plans to raise up to ₹5,500 crore.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    SpiceJet has been cancelling about one in five of its flights since August, according to official data. No SpiceJet flight left Mumbai for more than 12 hours from the evening of 17 November, stranding hundreds of passengers. It is reported to be flying only six aircraft and just over 20 flights a day, and media reports say it is behind on payments to suppliers, aircraft lessors and staff. It has not published financial results since the December 2025 quarter.

    Why it matters

    Passengers face last-minute cancellations, long delays and slow refunds. The aviation minister said in September that the regulator was reviewing SpiceJet's operations and finances.

    What’s next

    SpiceJet's reply to the DGCA's is due around 24 November; none had been reported by 23 November. On 18 November its board approved raising up to ₹5,500 ; shareholders and regulators still have to approve it, and no investors have been named. The BSE will suspend trading in its shares from 10 December unless it files overdue results by 7 December.

    14. 18 Nov2026
      18 Nov 2026 Travel New since your last visit Confirmed · SpiceJet filing

      Board approves raising up to ₹5,500 crore

      SpiceJet's board approved raising up to ₹5,500 , in one or more rounds, through new shares, debt with warrants or other securities, the airline said in an exchange filing reported by Business Today, Angel One and Upstox. Routes could include a share sale to institutions (QIP), a rights issue or a preferential allotment. The board also approved raising the company's authorised share capital, the maximum it can issue, from ₹2,000 crore to ₹7,500 crore. Both need shareholder and regulatory approval. Chairman Ajay Singh said the fundraise is 'an important opportunity to rebuild the airline'. The board was told about talks with possible new investors and asked management to close them quickly; no investors were named. The shares rose 5% to ₹9.97, the daily limit.

    16. 20 Nov2026
      20 Nov 2026 Travel New since your last visit Reported · PTI

      DGCA issues a show-cause notice; shares hit the lower limit

      India's aviation regulator, the DGCA, has asked SpiceJet to explain why action should not be taken against it, PTI reports, citing a notice dated 17 November. A show-cause notice is a formal warning that gives a company a chance to respond before any penalty. According to the reports, the notice says SpiceJet 'has failed to submit a credible plan for return to service of the grounded aircraft' and that most of its fleet is grounded. It also cites cancellations, poor on-time performance, not flying its approved schedule, uncertainty over renewing mandatory insurance, and concerns raised by overseas regulators about unpaid service providers. SpiceJet has seven days to reply. Its shares fell 5%, the most allowed in a day, to ₹9.74 on the BSE, per Upstox. SpiceJet had not commented, and the BSE asked it for clarification.

      SpiceJet reported to be flying 10–15 flights a day, while tickets stay on sale for cancelled routes

      SpiceJet is flying about 10–15 flights a day, against about 66 a day in its winter schedule, the Free Press Journal reports. At least eight Delhi–Mumbai flights each way were cancelled from 19 to 21 November, and a Mumbai–Dubai flight that had not flown for about a week was still on sale, it says. More than 7,000 complaints are on the government's AirSewa portal, PTI reports, per Millennium Post. SpiceJet has not commented on the ticket sales. No public reply to the DGCA's had been reported by 23 November.

      23–26 Nov 2026 Policy New since your last visit Reported · BusinessLine, NewsBytes, Live New Age

      SpiceJet reported to be down to six aircraft and just over 20 flights a day

      SpiceJet is flying six aircraft, two Q400 turboprops and four Boeing 737s, and just over 20 flights a day, BusinessLine reports, citing sources. Its main routes are Delhi–Mumbai, Delhi–Srinagar, Delhi–Dharamshala and Patna–Darbhanga, per Live New Age. Promoter Ajay Singh met civil aviation ministry officials, BusinessLine says. The airline is reported to be paying airports and fuel suppliers in cash upfront. The government's AirSewa portal listed 8,671 complaints against SpiceJet as of 22 November, up from 7,674 on 19 November. The pilots' union ALPA India has raised safety concerns linked to delayed salaries. SpiceJet has not publicly commented on the fleet figure.
