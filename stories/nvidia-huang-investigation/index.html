    Jensen Huang resigns: how the Nvidia espionage reports unfolded

    In two weeks, a report that Nvidia’s founder was targeted by Chinese intelligence led to a board inquiry, another allegation, a share slide and his resignation.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO on 12 November, two weeks after The New York Times reported that US intelligence found he had a close relationship with a Megaspeed executive they concluded was working for Beijing. Huang admitted infidelity, the board hired outside investigators, and a second alleged relationship, with a senior Nvidia executive, was reported on 11 November. On 19 November California's attorney general subpoenaed Nvidia over possible chip leaks to China and investor disclosures. On 20 November Reuters reported that SEC staff are examining possible accounting issues at Nvidia, and its shares fell 14%.

    Why it matters

    Nvidia makes the chips behind most of the world’s AI and is one of the most valuable companies on earth. Its founder leaving under investigation raises questions about US–China chip security, who runs the company next, and what it means for investors.

    What’s next

    Huang said on 17 November he would return as CEO if the board asked; its chair said the same day that it does not intend to reinstate him. Huang is due to be questioned behind closed doors by three House committees on 1 December, per Axios. A new board committee will oversee national security and export controls, and the board is searching for a permanent CEO. No details of the reported SEC inquiry have been made public.

      Reports say Huang is discussing a comeback; Bessent calls for an outside CEO

      The Financial Times reported on Sunday, citing people familiar with the matter, that Jensen Huang has discussed with confidants the possibility of returning as Nvidia's chief executive, according to Reuters and The Next Web. Two Nvidia investors, Altimeter's Brad Gerstner and Atreides' Gavin Baker, posted a letter to the board on X urging a slow search for a successor that includes "the potential return of Jensen Huang", The Next Web reports. The same day, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Fox News' "Sunday Morning Futures" that Nvidia needs a "dramatic shakeup" and should look outside the company for a new CEO. "If this were a bank and they hadn't complied like this, the OCC would be in there right now and a lot of the executives would be removed," he said, per Fortune. Fortune reports he also questioned whether Nvidia used "seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings." Nvidia did not immediately respond, per Fortune.

      Board reportedly not considering Huang's return; Trump says he 'would not mind'

      Nvidia's board is not considering bringing Jensen Huang back as CEO, according to people familiar with the matter who spoke to CNBC, the New York Post and Fox News on Monday (US time). An online petition to reinstate Huang has about 180,000 signatures, per CNBC. Fox News reports that Megaspeed says the executive left the company in 2025.

      First results without Huang: revenue doubles; board chair says Huang won't return

      Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for August–October, up 103% from a year earlier, in its first results under interim CEO Colette Kress. Data centre sales, mostly AI chips, were $107.6 billion. Profit was $65.3 billion. Nvidia expects about $130 billion in sales next quarter, assuming no data centre chip sales to China. "Our momentum is real," Kress said. On the earnings call, board chair Harvey Jones said "the Nvidia board does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO", per Constellation Research; CBS News, citing a person familiar with the board's thinking, also reports the board is against his return. Shares fell 0.44% in regular trading, then rose nearly 3% after hours, per CBS News.

    23. 19 Nov 2026 (US time)2026
      19 Nov 2026 (US time) Policy New since your last visit Confirmed · California Department of Justice

      California's attorney general subpoenas Nvidia

      California Attorney General Rob Bonta issued an investigative subpoena to Nvidia, his office said on Thursday, per AP and CBS News. A subpoena is a legal order to hand over documents or answer questions. The state wants to know whether Nvidia's advanced chips or confidential information may have reached China, and whether the company misled investors and the public, per AP; CBS adds whether it broke state law. "Companies cannot mislead their investors," Bonta said. "Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation." Nvidia did not immediately respond, per AP. A subpoena is not a finding of wrongdoing.

      Reuters: SEC staff examining possible accounting issues at Nvidia; shares fall 14%

      Two people briefed on the matter told Reuters that enforcement staff at the SEC, the US markets regulator, are examining "potential accounting issues" at Nvidia. Reuters could not find out when the inquiry began or which issues it covers. The Wall Street Journal had reported an SEC probe on 17 November without saying what it focused on. The SEC declined to comment and Nvidia did not immediately respond, per Reuters. An inquiry is not a finding of wrongdoing, and many end without action. Nvidia shares closed down 14.01% at $125.53, per Yahoo Finance and CappNotes, the worst day since January 2025, per CappNotes. Reuters, citing LSEG data, says shares were already down more than 6% before its report.
