    Air India 171: the crash investigation, step by step

    What the investigators have found so far about the June 2025 Ahmedabad crash that killed 260 people, what they have not, and why the final report is taking so long.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Air India flight 171, a Boeing 787-8 from Ahmedabad to London Gatwick, crashed about 32 seconds after take-off. A preliminary report found that the switches that send fuel to both engines moved to the off position about three seconds after lift-off, one second apart. It did not say why or how.

    Why it matters

    It is the deadliest crash of a Boeing 787 and the type's first fatal accident. The investigation, by India's Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB), decides what changes airlines, Boeing and regulators may have to make. Its purpose is safety, not deciding who is to blame or who pays compensation.

    What’s next

    On 18 November the government told the Supreme Court the investigation was at the 'fag end' and asked for two to three more weeks. It had said the draft final report would be filed in a sealed cover in October; reports of the hearing do not say whether that happened. The draft goes to the US safety board for comments before the final report is published.

      Flight 171 crashes seconds after take-off

      The Boeing 787-8, bound for London Gatwick, crashes about 32 seconds after taking off from Ahmedabad and hits the campus of BJ Medical College. 241 of the 242 people on board die, including all 12 crew, along with 19 people on the ground; one passenger survives. Former Gujarat chief minister Vijay Rupani is among the victims. The Tata Group, which owns Air India, announces a voluntary payment to each victim's family.

      Preliminary report: both fuel switches moved to CUTOFF

      The AAIB's 15-page preliminary report says that about three seconds after lift-off, the fuel control switches for both engines moved from RUN to CUTOFF, one second apart, starving the engines of fuel. They were moved back to RUN about 10 and 14 seconds later and one engine began to relight, but the plane was too low to recover. On the cockpit recording, one pilot asks the other, "Why did you cut off?" and the other says he did not; the report does not say who said what. It finds the plane airworthy, notes that Air India had not done a 2018 FAA-advised check of the switch locks because it was not mandatory, and makes no safety recommendations.

      FAA: fuel switch design is not unsafe

      In a notice to other countries' regulators dated 11 July, the US Federal Aviation Administration says the fuel switch design, including its lock, is not an unsafe condition that would require an airworthiness directive on any Boeing model, including the 787. Boeing refers questions to the notice.

      One year on: "significant progress", no date for the report

      On the eve of the first anniversary, the AAIB says it has made significant progress analysing aircraft systems, flight recorder data, engine parts and maintenance records, but gives no timeline.       AAIB to Supreme Court: draft report around October

      In an affidavit, the AAIB says it has completed 49 of 66 required investigation steps, that remaining work should take about six weeks, and that a draft final report is expected around October 2026. Analysis of engine monitoring data and of organisational factors is still under way. The draft must first go to the US National Transportation Safety Board for comments. The AAIB argues a court-monitored inquiry is not needed because its investigation follows international rules and is about safety, not blame.

    What this means for you

    The cause has not been established. No regulator has ordered changes to Boeing 787s: the preliminary report made no recommendations, and the US Federal Aviation Administration said in July 2025 that the fuel switch design is not an unsafe condition. The final report is what will say why the crash happened and what should change.
