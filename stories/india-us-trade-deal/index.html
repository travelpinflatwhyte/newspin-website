    India–US trade deal: announced in February, still not signed

    A tariff cut was announced with fanfare. Nine months later, courts, new duties and a sanctions law have kept it in limbo.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    The US and India announced a deal in February to cut US on Indian goods to 18% from 50%. On 18 November India's Commerce Secretary said the deal is "more or less finalised" but cannot be signed until the US sets its new tariff structure.

    Why it matters

    US tariffs decide how competitive Indian exports like textiles, gems, engineering goods and pharma are in India’s biggest export market.

    What’s next

    The US has proposed a tariff of up to 25% on Indian goods, cut to 17% with a trade deal; comments are due by 5 January and hearings start on 25 January. On 27 November Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal said the deal is "close to culmination" but gave no signing date. A former senior US diplomat says both sides may aim to close phase one around the summit in Florida in December.

    8. 18 Nov2026
      18 Nov 2026 Trade New since your last visit Stated · Commerce Secretary · not signed

      Commerce Secretary: deal 'more or less finalised' but can't be signed until new US tariff rules are in place

      Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal said "every aspect of the deal has been more or less finalised" and negotiations will conclude soon. But he said the two countries cannot sign it until Washington puts its new tariff structure in place: "Till that tariff architecture is in place, the countries won't be able to sign the deal." The US is rebuilding its tariffs after its Supreme Court ruled in February that the emergency law used for the earlier tariffs did not allow them. Agrawal also pointed to pending US Section 301 investigations, saying countries will want to know their outcome before signing. He spoke at a FICCI event in New Delhi, per ANI.

    10. 24 Nov2026
      24 Nov 2026 Trade New since your last visit Reported · Wall Street Journal (via PTI, IBTimes); not announced

      Report: US may raise tariffs on India and others as soon as this week

      The US may announce new tariffs on China, India and other countries as early as this week, the Wall Street Journal reported, according to PTI and IBTimes. They would follow a probe the US Trade Representative opened in March into 16 economies. It looks at whether government subsidies helped firms make far more than they can sell, flooding world markets with cheap goods. IBTimes, citing the Journal, says India and Vietnam could face higher duties, while Japan, South Korea and the EU could stay at 15% or less under their trade deals. No rates have been set, and the timing could change. It is one of the pending 'Section 301' probes that India's Commerce Secretary said must be settled before the deal can be signed. No Indian government response was reported. For exporters, a higher US duty would make Indian goods dearer in their biggest market.

    11. 25 Nov2026
      25 Nov 2026 Trade New since your last visit Proposed · USTR

      US proposes a tariff of up to 25% on Indian goods, or 17% with a trade deal

      The US Trade Representative proposed a tariff of up to 25% on Indian goods, after finding that India's excess factory capacity is an unfair practice. USTR says the rate could be cut to 17% if India reaches a reciprocal trade agreement. It counts the 10% duty India already faces since July rather than adding to it, and some goods would be exempt. India is one of 14 economies covered; Vietnam faces up to 35% and the EU, Japan and South Korea 15%. Nothing takes effect yet: comments are due by 5 January and hearings start on 25 January.

    12. 25 Nov2026
      25 Nov 2026 Trade New since your last visit Stated · Commerce Ministry

      India says it has no 'structural excess capacity' and wants tariff concerns settled in the trade talks

      India's Commerce Ministry responded to the proposed US tariff of up to 25%. It said the US notice "is currently under examination" and that "India does not have structural excess capacity in any sector". It said India "engaged constructively" throughout the US investigation. The ministry said trade concerns "should be addressed within the framework of these negotiations", meaning the bilateral trade deal announced in February. It said the government remains committed to working closely with the US to conclude the deal early. No date for signing was given. The think tank GTRI said the new tariffs "recreate the reciprocal-tariff regime under a new name", per the Free Press Journal.

    13. 26 Nov2026
      26 Nov 2026 Trade New since your last visit Stated · Delhi Police, SKM

      Delhi Police refuse permission for farmers' march; protests against the trade deal planned nationwide

      The farmers' coalition Samyukt Kisan Morcha (SKM) called protests in Delhi and about 100 other places on 26 November. One of its demands is that the government "reject the India-US free trade agreement and other free trade agreements", per National Herald. Other demands include a legal guarantee of minimum support prices () and a loan waiver. Delhi Police said "no application for permission has been received and no permission has been granted" for a march to Jantar Mantar, per India TV (IANS). Police said prohibitory orders are in force in the New Delhi district and that violations will invite legal action. Reports of turnout or detentions were not available at the time of writing.

      Goyal says the US trade deal is 'close to culmination'

      Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal said on 27 November that "the US trade deal is close to culmination", speaking online to an industry conference, per Reuters. He said "the process and pathway, which was always something which held back finalising the deal, is now taking some shape." He gave no date for signing. The remarks come two days after the US proposed a of up to 25% on Indian goods, which would be capped at 17% if a deal is reached.
