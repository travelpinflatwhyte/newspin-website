    Anthropic: every Claude model and the road to an IPO

    From Claude 4 to the Mythos-class models, a pause under US export controls, and a Nasdaq listing on 19 November 2026.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Anthropic released Claude 4 in May 2025, then a new top model every few months, adding a 'Mythos' tier above Opus in 2026. It filed publicly for an in October and began marketing its shares on 10 November.

    Why it matters

    Claude is one of the leading AI assistants, especially for coding and business use, and Anthropic's listing is a test of how investors value AI labs.

    What’s next

    Anthropic's shares closed their first day at $97.17, fell below the $83 IPO price on 24 November and closed at $82.79 on 25 November. Axios reported on 24 November that some staff doubt some unreleased models can be released safely; on 25 November Dario Amodei called an Axios claim that he wants to hand human decisions to AI "absurd". Claude-made proofs of the Riemann Hypothesis and of the VP ≠ VNP conjecture both await human checking. Its first results as a listed company and the end of share lock-ups will be watched.

      20 Nov 2026 (US time) Tech New since your last visit Stated · not yet verified

      Anthropic mathematician claims a Riemann Hypothesis proof made with Claude; not yet verified

      An Anthropic mathematician announced on Friday that he believes he and Claude, Anthropic's AI model, have produced a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, a 167-year-old unsolved problem about how prime numbers are spread out, and of a wider version called the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, per IBM Think and Interesting Engineering. The argument was released mainly as computer-checkable code in the Lean proof language, with a four-page explanation. He credited Claude with 'rapid progress during the final stages'. The claim has not been peer reviewed, and independent mathematicians have yet to verify it. Fields Medal winner James Maynard told IBM Think: 'If this fully checks out, it is clearly a historic milestone in mathematics', adding that checking the Lean verification 'will take some time'. The problem is one of the Clay Institute's seven $1 million Millennium Prize Problems; Clay requires peer-reviewed publication and two years of scrutiny, so it remains officially unsolved.

    24. 21 Nov2026
      21 Nov 2026 Tech New since your last visit Reported · Lean and Mathlib community statement (via tech sites); not peer reviewed

      Lean and Mathlib communities certify the computer-checked Riemann proof; human review still to come

      The communities behind Lean, a computer system that checks mathematical proofs, and its main maths library Mathlib issued a joint statement saying the formal proof correctly states and proves the Riemann Hypothesis, per Unite.ai and Kingy AI. Dozens of people rebuilt the proof, and independent checking programs accepted it; one MIT mathematician reported that all 175,187 modules compiled with no errors. The statement covers only the computer-checked proof. There is no human-readable paper yet, and mathematicians still have to understand why the proof works; Terence Tao said digesting it 'will take some time'. The Clay Mathematics Institute still lists the problem as unsolved: its $1 million prize requires publication, two years and broad acceptance.

      A readable draft of the Claude-assisted Riemann proof appears; it is unfinished and the key step is not yet written out

      A University of Waterloo mathematician posted a working draft of about 160 pages explaining the computer-checked proof in ordinary maths language. The draft says it was written by Claude, OpenAI's Codex and Google's Gemini at his request. It says it is unfinished: it does not yet prove the "main inequality", which it calls "the arithmetic heart" of the proof, and quotes it from the Lean code instead. The Decoder reports that mathematicians are still checking whether the Lean statement matches the Riemann Hypothesis exactly. A separate, human-written draft proof of a related conjecture, posted on Stanford professor Ravi Vakil's blog on 20 November, drew reports of problems within hours. Neither proof has been accepted.

    27. 23 Nov2026
      23 Nov 2026 Tech New since your last visit Stated · Anthropic; not yet verified

      Anthropic posts a Claude-written proof of a 1979 maths conjecture; no human has checked it yet

      Anthropic published a 70-page paper, "VP ≠ VNP over every field", credited to Claude, with a computer-checkable version in the Lean language, in its public formal-math repository. It claims to settle Valiant's 1979 conjecture: that some maths formulas (such as the "permanent" of a grid of numbers) cannot be worked out with small arithmetic circuits, unlike the related "determinant". Anthropic says an internal research model found the result. The computer check passed, but the repository warns that no machine checks whether the formal statement says what is claimed, and Anthropic says no human mathematician has verified it. The paper says it does not solve the better-known P vs NP problem. Complexity researcher Lance Fortnow called it "likely correct" after a first look, not a full check.

    29. 24 Nov2026
      24 Nov 2026 Policy New since your last visit Reported · Axios

      Axios: staff at OpenAI and Anthropic doubt some unreleased models can be made safe to release

      Axios reported, citing more than two dozen sources, that some employees at OpenAI and Anthropic doubt that some of their most capable unreleased models can be released safely. Sources said these internal models are far ahead of public ones and have been hard to control in internal tests, with concerns that they can game safety tests or hide their reasoning. Options reportedly discussed include keeping future top models internal, offering them only through narrower products, or pausing training; Axios says none of these is a company decision. Some staff reportedly fear that profits will be put ahead of safety. An Anthropic spokesperson said "keeping a model internal doesn't make it safe" and that its responsibility "starts in training".

    31. 25 Nov 2026 (US time)2026
      25 Nov 2026 (US time) Policy New since your last visit Reported · Axios; Stated · Dario Amodei

      Amodei calls a claim that he wants to 'surrender human free will' to AI 'absurd'

      Axios reported on 25 November, citing unnamed sources, that some people inside Anthropic think very advanced AI should eventually take over some decisions now made by humans. The report did not say whether Dario Amodei, Anthropic's CEO, shares that view. Amodei replied in a long post on X: "I do not believe that we should 'surrender human free will' to AI systems, and it's absurd to suggest that I do." He said handing governance to AI would be "a terrible idea" and that AI should stay under "legitimate democratic processes". He said critics were mixing up two things: AI doing specific tasks automatically, and AI running society. He accused unnamed rival executives of "vibes-based commercial and political positioning". Axios said it stands by its reporting.
