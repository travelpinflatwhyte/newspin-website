    Starlink: a million-satellite plan, India's long wait and the fight over Ukraine

    SpaceX won US approval for up to a million data-centre satellites, while Starlink still waits to launch in India and faces disputes in Ukraine and South Africa.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Starlink is SpaceX's satellite internet service, with over 11,100 satellites in and 13.6 million home subscribers. On 25 November the US Federal Communications Commission () approved a separate SpaceX plan for up to 1,000,000 satellites that would work as data centres for AI in orbit. In India, Starlink got its licence in June 2025 but has not launched.

    Why it matters

    Starlink can bring internet to villages and remote areas that cables and towers do not reach. A million more satellites would crowd orbit; astronomers say they would spoil the night sky and telescope images. In Ukraine, the army relies on the service for drones and communications.

    What’s next

    In India, Starlink, Eutelsat OneWeb and Jio's satellite venture are waiting for security clearance and a final decision on spectrum charges; a panel backed 5% of revenue in September, but the Cabinet has not signed off. No Indian price has been announced. SpaceX must put its first Starmind satellite in orbit by November 2033.

      India sets security rules for satellite internet companies

      The Department of Telecommunications (DoT) said satellite internet firms may not process Indian users' data abroad or connect Indian users through facilities outside India. Lawful interception, which lets security agencies monitor traffic, must be in place before services start. Each ground station (gateway) in India needs its own security clearance, and blocked websites must also be blocked on satellite services.

    3. 9 May2025
      9 May 2025 Policy New since your last visit Confirmed · TRAI

      Regulator TRAI recommends a 4% charge on satellite internet revenue

      The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) recommended that satellite operators pay 4% of their adjusted gross revenue (AGR) for the use of spectrum, the radio frequencies they need. Operators of moving low-orbit satellites, such as Starlink, would also pay ₹500 a year for each urban customer; rural users would be exempt. Spectrum would be handed out by the government rather than auctioned, for five years.

    7. 30 Jan2026
      30 Jan 2026 Tech New since your last visit Filed · SpaceX

      SpaceX asks to launch up to a million satellites as orbiting AI data centres

      A SpaceX application to the US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) became public, asking to launch up to 1,000,000 solar-powered satellites that would work as data centres for artificial intelligence. They would fly 500 to 2,000 km up and pass data between each other by laser. SpaceX asked to skip the usual round that lets rivals file competing plans. The filing says the project is "a first step towards becoming a Kardashev II-level civilization", a term for a civilisation that harnesses its star's energy.

      Amazon asks the FCC to reject SpaceX's plan

      Amazon, which is building its own satellite network, petitioned the FCC to deny the application. It called it "a speculative placeholder rather than a complete application" with no plan for avoiding collisions at that scale, and warned of "an orbital monopoly". On 11 March Carr replied on X that Amazon "should focus on the fact that it will fall roughly 1,000 satellites short" of its own deployment target.

    10. 16 Mar2026
      16 Mar 2026 World New since your last visit Stated · Royal Astronomical Society, SpaceX

      Astronomers warn of damage to the night sky; SpaceX rejects the objections

      The UK's Royal Astronomical Society said "deploying more than one million exceptionally bright satellites would utterly destroy" dark skies and urged the FCC to reject the plan. It said thousands would be visible to the naked eye. An astronomer at the European Southern Observatory told Space.com its Very Large Telescope could lose up to 10% of the pixels in its images. The same day SpaceX filed a reply to objectors including Amazon, Blue Origin, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society, saying the system "seeks no exclusive use of any spectrum or orbital resources".

    25. 25 Nov2026
      25 Nov 2026 Law New since your last visit Confirmed · FCC

      FCC approves SpaceX's million-satellite 'Starmind' data-centre network

      The authorised SpaceX to launch up to 1,000,000 "Starmind" satellites that work as data centres in orbit, calling it "the first authorization in U.S. history for a constellation of data centers in space". It approved altitudes of 500 to 1,002 km, lower than the 2,000 km SpaceX asked for. SpaceX must have one working satellite by November 2033 and the full network by November 2040. The FCC put off SpaceX's request to dispose of higher satellites away from Earth instead of letting them burn up, and said concerns about astronomy were outside this decision. Carr said it "opens a bold new frontier"; the American Astronomical Society said it was "disheartened and dismayed".

    What this means for you

    In India, Starlink is not yet on sale, and no official price exists; figures that appeared on its website in December 2025 were withdrawn as test data. When it launches, it is likely to matter most where broadband and mobile signals are weak. Jio and Airtel have deals to sell its equipment.
