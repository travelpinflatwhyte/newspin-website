    West Asia war, the Strait of Hormuz and India’s oil bill

    How the US–Iran conflict disrupted the world’s most important oil route, and why it matters so much to India.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Since the war began, shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has been disrupted. Brent peaked near $118 in late October, then fell sharply in November as Qatar mediated talks between Iran and the US, before rebounding on fresh attacks.

    Why it matters

    India imports most of its crude oil, and a large share of its oil, LNG and almost all its LPG has passed through Hormuz. The government says it absorbed ₹43 of a needed ₹50-a-litre diesel increase.

    What’s next

    Brent fell to about $86.7 on 23 November, its lowest since late August, as Gulf exports held up despite weekend attacks. Iran says it will confront the US blockade but that Hormuz could be safe quickly if the US meets its terms. President Trump has said he could resume strikes after the new year if talks fail. OPEC+ meets on 29 November.

      Iran fires drones at a US base in Bahrain, twice in three days

      Iran's Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) say they sent six drones at Riffa Air Base in Bahrain on Sunday, aiming at US military equipment, in retaliation for US strikes on Iran, including on Qeshm Island. It was the second such attack in three days: on Friday the IRGC said it hit a US early-warning radar there. Bahrain says its air defences intercepted the drones. The US military's Central Command (CENTCOM) said it shot down two drones on Friday with no injuries or damage, and a spokesman said there were "no impacts to American forces" on Sunday. The Washington Post says Bahrain had been relatively quiet for months. Iran's foreign ministry said Tehran would keep responding to "military aggression". On Monday morning in India, Brent crude was trading about 1% lower at $94.85 a barrel, PTI reported.

      Tankers hit near Hormuz; Iran strikes at a Kuwait base; Riyadh tower damaged

      An ADNOC product tanker was hit by an 'unknown projectile' north of Khasab, Oman, at the mouth of the Strait of Hormuz, leaving one seafarer missing and 11 injured, ADNOC's shipping arm confirmed, per Seatrade Maritime. A second tanker was hit in its engine room near Khasab on 18 November; its crew are safe, per Gulf News and Arab News. Nobody has claimed either attack. On 17 November a US fighter struck what a US official called an Iranian missile launch site on Qeshm Island in 'self-defense', after Iranian drones targeted US forces in Bahrain, per TIME. On 18 November Iran attacked Ali Al-Salem Air Base in Kuwait, which hosts US forces, and targets in Bahrain; both countries said they repelled the attacks, per The Hill and TIME. Iranian military units separately claimed that tankers hit mines east of Hormuz; this has not been independently confirmed. On 19 November a Houthi attack damaged the Public Investment Fund tower in Riyadh, with no injuries reported, per Al Jazeera and The National. Despite this, Brent settled at $91.95 on 18 November, down 1.28%, as US stockpiles rose, Reuters reports.

      Two tankers hit leaving Hormuz; Iran fires missiles and drones at US bases in Bahrain

      Early on Saturday two tankers leaving the Gulf were hit by unknown projectiles, one about 20 km north of Khasab in Oman and one about 31 km east of it, said UKMTO, the British agency that tracks shipping. One was hit in the stern and one in the engine room; no one was reported hurt. Iran's navy said a tanker caught fire after hitting a mine in a restricted zone east of the strait; it is not clear if this was one of the two. On Saturday night Iran launched drones and ballistic missiles at US bases in Bahrain, per Iranian media cited by Euronews. Bahrain said its air defences intercepted several attacks and a fire broke out at a civilian industrial site, with no injuries. The Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) said they hit two Patriot missile batteries and a radar; Bahrain and the US have not confirmed this. The US military denied a separate IRGC claim that Iran damaged a US F-16 jet on Friday. Iran's deputy foreign minister said more than 14 Iranian tankers are ready to sail through the US blockade to China, and warned of a 'fierce military response' if they are stopped.

      Iran says two supertankers hit mines east of Hormuz; UKMTO reports a tanker 'incident with military forces'

      Iran's navy said on Sunday that two oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines east of the Strait of Hormuz, saying the ships had switched off their tracking systems and entered a 'forbidden' area, per Xinhua. The Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) navy separately said two supertankers exploded on 'unauthorized' routes north of Oman's Al Ghanam Island. The ships were not named and no one else has confirmed the incident. On Sunday evening UKMTO, the British agency that tracks shipping, said a tanker 'was involved in an incident with military forces in the Gulf of Oman', near Chabahar, and that investigations were under way, per Euronews. It gave no ship name or casualties. Euronews says the area is near the US blockade line, but it is not known whether this involved one of the Iranian tankers said to be ready to test the blockade. The IRGC also said it shot down a US MQ-1 drone east of the strait; the US has not commented.

      Brent falls to its lowest since late August as Gulf exports hold up

      Brent crude settled near $86.6–86.8 a barrel on Monday, its lowest since late August, down from $92.57 on Friday. US crude (WTI) closed around $79–80. Lower oil prices help India, which imports most of its crude.

      Iran vows to confront the US blockade; says Hormuz could be safe again if its terms are met

      Iran's foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said Iran would "definitely not tolerate" the US naval blockade and would "use all our tools" to confront it, per ABC News. He said security in the Strait of Hormuz could be restored quickly if the US lifts sanctions, ends the blockade and carries out the terms of the June memorandum of understanding. Oil shipped through Hormuz averaged about 9.5 million barrels a day in the 10 days to 19 November, down from 12.2 million in October, per Kpler data cited by Reuters. Iran began rationing electricity on 22 November because of fuel shortages, ABC reports.
