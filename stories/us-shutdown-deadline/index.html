    US shutdown watch: the 11 December funding deadline

    Three US shutdowns in a year, a stopgap that runs out on 11 December, and a fight over money for the Iran war.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Most of the US government is funded by a stopgap law that runs out on 11 December. In the past year the US has had a 43-day full shutdown and a record 76-day shutdown of Homeland Security.

    Why it matters

    In a shutdown, unfunded agencies stop non-essential work and many federal workers go unpaid, including air traffic controllers and airport screeners, which has meant flight cuts and long security queues before.

    What’s next

    Congress must pass another stopgap or full-year spending bills by 11 December, and has left for Thanksgiving with no deal. The White House has sent its requests for the next stopgap, which Punchbowl reports could run to 31 March. Some Democrats want limits on Iran war funding; the new Congress meets in January.

    1. 1 Oct – 12 Nov2025
      1 Oct – 12 Nov 2025 Policy New since your last visit Official data · Congress

      The longest full shutdown ends after 43 days

      US government funding ran out on 1 October 2025 after Congress failed to agree a budget, mainly over whether to extend health insurance subsidies under the Affordable Care Act. Hundreds of thousands of federal workers were sent home without pay; the Government Shutdown Tracker puts the number at about 670,000 or more. About 13,000 air traffic controllers kept working unpaid, and on 7 November the ordered flight cuts at 40 major airports. The Senate passed a deal 60–40 on 10 November and the House 222–209 on 12 November; President Trump signed it the same day. It funded most agencies only until 30 January 2026 and guaranteed back pay.

    2. 31 Jan – 3 Feb2026
      31 Jan – 3 Feb 2026 Policy New since your last visit Official data · Congress

      A four-day partial shutdown ends; Homeland Security split off

      Funding for about half of the government lapsed at midnight on 30 January 2026, after Senate Democrats withdrew support for funding the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) over immigration enforcement. The Senate passed a $1.2 trillion package 71–29 that funded five spending bills to 30 September but gave DHS only a two-week extension. The House approved it 217–214 on 3 February, the shutdown's fourth day, and President Trump signed it that afternoon. The law guarantees back pay for furloughed workers.

      Homeland Security shutdown begins

      DHS funding ran out at 12:01 am US Eastern time on 14 February after Democrats blocked a second two-week extension, amid disputes over immigration enforcement. The rest of the government stayed open. Airport security screeners at the Transportation Security Administration (TSA), the Coast Guard and other DHS staff had to keep working without pay.

      Trump orders TSA workers paid as screeners quit

      With airport screeners missing paychecks, President Trump signed an order on 27 March to pay TSA workers from funds in last year's One Big Beautiful Bill Act, per HSToday and Wikipedia. The same day the Senate approved a deal funding DHS except Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and Customs and Border Protection (CBP), but House Speaker Mike Johnson refused a House vote on it. TSA's acting head told Congress of 'nearly $1 billion in delayed paychecks', per HSToday.

    5. 30 Apr2026
      30 Apr 2026 Policy New since your last visit Official data · Congress

      Record 76-day Homeland Security shutdown ends, without ICE and CBP

      The House passed the Senate's DHS bill by voice vote on 30 April and President Trump signed it, ending a 76-day lapse that HSToday calls the longest agency shutdown in US history. The law funds 20 of DHS's 22 agencies, including TSA, FEMA, the Coast Guard and the Secret Service, to 30 September. ICE and CBP were left out, to be funded through a separate budget bill that Republicans can pass without Democratic votes.

      With the new budget year starting on 1 October, the House passed a stopgap funding law, known as a , 370–48 on 1 September, and President Trump signed it on 2 September. It keeps spending at last year's levels until 11 December. Harvard's federal relations office says the Senate had passed it 90–6 in August.

    7. 12–20 Nov2026
      12–20 Nov 2026 Policy New since your last visit Stated · lawmakers

      Iran war splits Democrats ahead of the 11 December deadline

      Some Democrats say they will not back another funding law unless it limits money for the war with Iran. Senator Chris Van Hollen, with Senators Duckworth, Sanders and Bennet, said any deal 'must include language that restricts the Trump Administration from using appropriated funds to continue his war against Iran', per CBS News. Others warn against a shutdown: Rep. Rosa DeLauro, the top Democrat on the House Appropriations Committee, favours a short extension to February and said Americans 'may not believe that shutting the government down ends the war'. Democratic leaders Hakeem Jeffries and Chuck Schumer have not set out their approach. The administration is considering a stopgap running through March, Roll Call reported, per the Tracker. A funding law needs 60 votes in the Senate. A White House official said the economic pressure on Iran means the President has no 'need to conduct major combat operations'.

    8. 20–23 Nov2026
      20–23 Nov 2026 Policy New since your last visit Reported

      Congress leaves for Thanksgiving with no deal; White House sends its list for the next stopgap

      Congress left for the Thanksgiving break without a funding deal. Of the 12 yearly spending bills, the House has passed three and the Senate none, per The Center Square, and appropriators in both parties expect another short-term stopgap. On 23 November the White House sent Congress its list of 'anomalies', exceptions to a stopgap's freeze on spending, per SpacePolicyOnline. It asks for $5 billion for the Golden Dome missile-defence programme and $2 billion for NASA's Artemis Moon programme, among other items, per SpacePolicyOnline and Breaking Defense. Punchbowl reports the new stopgap would notionally run to 31 March, but the White House list gives no end date, SpacePolicyOnline says. A funding law needs 60 Senate votes, so at least seven Democrats must back it.

    What this means for you

    Flying to or within the US? During the last shutdowns air traffic controllers and TSA screeners worked unpaid, and in November 2025 the FAA cut flights at 40 major airports. If there is a shutdown, check your flight before you leave and allow extra time at security.
