    Russia and NATO: missiles, plots and closed borders on Europe's eastern edge

    Russian missiles have landed in Poland, Estonia is closing its border, Denmark has expelled a Russian diplomat, and Ukraine's war is reaching ships far from the front.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    A run of incidents linked to Russia has hit NATO and EU countries. Russian missiles came down in Poland in July and again on 20 November. Estonia blames Russian military intelligence for an arson attempt and is closing its road border with Russia. Denmark expelled Russia's defence attaché over an alleged murder plot. Ukraine says it hit a Russian warship in a Libyan port.

    Why it matters

    NATO's 32 members have promised to treat an attack on one as an attack on all. Each incident tests how the alliance responds without being drawn into the war. Border closures, air-raid alerts and expulsions also affect travel and trade in the region.

    What’s next

    Estonia closes its three road crossings with Russia from 1 December. Poland plans a no-fly zone along its eastern border, hopefully by late December or early January, and wants NATO to do the same along the eastern flank. NATO has sent an early-warning radar plane (AWACS) to Poland. A Russian drone briefly entered Polish airspace on 23 November. Russia says Estonia is "on the verge of triggering open war"; Estonia denies Russia's claims. Britain has written to about 100,000 former service members to update their details in case they are needed.

      Estonia blames Russian military intelligence for an arson attempt and moves to close its border

      Estonia's Internal Security Service said Russia's military intelligence (GRU) ordered an attempted arson attack on a drone maker near Tallinn on 14 November, the second such attempt on a defence company since August. Eight Latvian citizens were detained. On 15 November the government moved to close its three road crossings with Russia, at Narva, Luhamaa and Koidula, from 1 December. Russia denies involvement.

    4. 20 Nov2026
      20 Nov 2026 World New since your last visit Confirmed · Polish government, Lublin prosecutors

      Three Russian cruise missiles come down in south-east Poland

      Three objects fell in south-east Poland during a large Russian attack on Ukraine. Polish prosecutors in Lublin said on 22 November that all three were Kh-101 cruise missiles, per Notes from Poland. They were in Polish airspace for about three minutes, and no one was hurt. Prime Minister Tusk called it an apparent "intentional provocation", timed to a military exercise in Poland. NATO's Rutte would not say whether it was deliberate. Russia had not commented.

      Denmark expels Russia's defence attaché over an alleged plot to kill a Ukrainian-American rapper

      Denmark's intelligence service (PET) said Russian intelligence planned to have a Ukrainian-American rapper killed at a concert near Copenhagen, using a banned Danish gang. Two Danish men were charged with attempted murder; both reportedly deny it. Denmark gave Russia's defence attaché seven days to leave. Russia's embassy called the expulsion "vengeful" and said Denmark had shown no evidence.

      Drones hit Russian ships in the Gulf of Finland and in a Libyan port

      On 21 November drones hit the Russian cargo ship Sparta II in the Gulf of Finland; all 16 crew were evacuated, per Al Jazeera. Kyiv has not claimed that attack. On 22 November Ukrainian sea drones hit the Russian corvette Merkury at Benghazi, Libya, President Zelenskyy said the next day, about 2,400 km from the front line. Libya's eastern government asked the UN for an investigation.

      NATO sends a radar plane to Poland; Russia warns Estonia of 'open war'

      NATO ambassadors met in Brussels, and NATO's top military commander ordered an AWACS early-warning radar plane to Poland. NATO said Russia "bears full responsibility" for the missiles. Estonia denied letting Ukraine use its airspace.

    8. 23–24 Nov2026
      23–24 Nov 2026 World New since your last visit Confirmed · Polish military; Stated · Lt Gen Nowak (NATO jet)

      Russian drone briefly enters Polish airspace; Poland says a NATO jet was cleared to fire on the 20 November missile

      Late on 23 November a Russian drone "briefly violated Polish airspace" during attacks on western Ukraine, Poland's military said. Permission to shoot it down was given, but it was not fired on after it turned east, because of the risk to people on the ground, per ABC News. It flew over Dorohusk, a border town with a major rail and freight crossing, for several minutes. A deputy defence minister said Poland assumes it was deliberate; Russia's ambassador said no convincing evidence has been shown. Separately, Poland's operational commander, Lt Gen Ireneusz Nowak, said a Swedish Gripen jet under NATO command was cleared to fire at a Russian cruise missile on 20 November, but bad weather stopped the pilot finding it, per Fox News. Poland has again asked the US for permanent troops. Estonia's foreign minister wrote that closing the border "will limit opportunities for hostile intelligence activities", per TIME.

    9. 25 Nov2026
      25 Nov 2026 World New since your last visit Stated · Polish defence ministry

      Poland plans a no-fly zone along its eastern border to make it easier to shoot down Russian missiles

      Deputy Defence Minister Cezary Tomczyk said Poland will ask the government to set up a no-fly zone stretching "a dozen or a few dozen kilometres" in from its eastern border, per Notes from Poland and The Boston Report. It would help Polish pilots shoot down missiles and drones that stray in from Ukraine. A draft regulation has been submitted, and he hopes it will be in place by late December or early January. Poland will urge NATO to adopt the same rule along the eastern flank. Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski argued that shooting down missiles over Ukraine, at Kyiv's request, would be self-defence, but Tomczyk said "NATO is not in favour of this in any way". Former defence minister Mariusz Błaszczak, of the opposition Law and Justice party, called the plan "cosmetic".

    10. 26 Nov2026
      26 Nov 2026 World New since your last visit Stated · UK Defence Secretary

      Britain writes to about 100,000 former service members to update their details in case of a call-up

      Britain's defence ministry has written to about 100,000 former military personnel in its Strategic Reserve, people who can be recalled in a crisis, asking them to update their contact details, jobs and skills. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting called it a "sensible, necessary precaution" given the "threat environment in Europe", and said it "should be routine, but it hasn't happened for some time". The letters are not call-up orders. They also invite volunteers for training, including NATO's Steadfast Defender exercise in spring 2027, per Kyiv Post, citing The Telegraph. A bill before Parliament would raise the maximum recall age from 55 to 65, per Defence24.
