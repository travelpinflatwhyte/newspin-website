    xAI and Grok: models, the SpaceX merger and the missing Grok 5

    Elon Musk's AI company shipped Grok 3 to Grok 4.7, merged into SpaceX and bought Cursor. Grok 5 has slipped past every promised date.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    xAI released Grok 3 in February 2025 and Grok 4 in July 2025, then a run of Grok 4.x models through 2026. SpaceX acquired xAI in February 2026, and the company now uses SpaceX branding.

    Why it matters

    Grok is built into X and Tesla products, and xAI's huge Colossus computers make it one of the few labs able to train the largest models.

    What’s next

    Grok 5: xAI said in January it was in training; SpaceX has pointed to before the end of 2026. Virginia and Oregon's lawsuits over Grok deepfakes are at an early stage. SpaceX's Starmind computing satellites, approved by the on 25 November, must start operating by 2033.

    12. 22–23 Nov2026
      22–23 Nov 2026 Law New since your last visit Filed · not ruled on (allegations by the Virginia and Oregon attorneys general)

      Virginia and Oregon sue SpaceX over sexual deepfakes made with Grok

      The attorneys general of Virginia and Oregon sued SpaceX, which owns xAI and X, under consumer-protection laws. They allege Grok's "spicy mode" was used to make sexualised images of real people, including children, and that the companies misled users about safety. They seek penalties, repayments and court orders. SpaceX did not comment. X's safety team has said it blocks such requests and that users who made them broke its rules. The court has not ruled.

    13. 23 Nov2026
      23 Nov 2026 Tech New since your last visit Reported · The New York Times

      NYT: Musk told staff to 'sell compute because we can't compete'

      The New York Times reports that in August Elon Musk renamed a SpaceX AI group chat of hundreds of engineers "Sell compute because we can't compete", and warned that anyone "wasting GPUs" while the company was "failing to compete" would be asked to leave. Musk told staff that renting out its Colossus computers to rivals had become more lucrative than using them for SpaceX's own AI products, per the Times. It reports that Anthropic is now SpaceX's biggest customer, Google is also a client, and the AI unit accounts for more than half of SpaceX's revenue. SpaceX did not comment, per Forbes.

    14. 25 Nov 2026 (US time)2026
      25 Nov 2026 (US time) Tech New since your last visit Confirmed · FCC

      US regulator clears SpaceX to launch up to a million AI computing satellites

      The Federal Communications Commission (), which licenses US satellites, authorised SpaceX's 'Starmind' network: up to one million satellites that would work as data centres in orbit, processing data such as AI tasks for use on Earth. The FCC called it the first US authorisation for a constellation of data centres in space. The first satellite must be working by 2033 and the full network by 2040, per GeekWire, with conditions including a plan to limit space debris. Amazon, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society had objected; the astronomers said they were "disheartened and dismayed". Critics also question whether SpaceX will ever launch that many; about 17,000 satellites from all operators are in orbit today, per Al Jazeera. Elon Musk said the network "will be a great asset to America and the world".
