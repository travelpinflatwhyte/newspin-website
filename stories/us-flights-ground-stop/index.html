    All US flights halted: the FAA ground stop, hour by hour

    Why every departure in the United States stopped for about four hours on Friday, what officials say caused it, and what it means if you are flying this weekend.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    On Friday afternoon US time (early Saturday in India), the Federal Aviation Administration (), which runs US air traffic control, stopped every flight from taking off at US airports after a communications failure hit several of its air traffic control centres. The stop was lifted about four hours later.

    Why it matters

    It was only the second nationwide since the 11 September 2001 attacks, and thousands of flights were cancelled. Planes and crews ended up in the wrong places, so delays and cancellations are expected through the weekend.

    What’s next

    Flights are back to normal. The root cause is still not known: the FAA has paused installing the router model involved, and officials and the router's maker disagree over whether the equipment or its set-up was at fault. FAA chief Bryan Bedford faces a House panel on 1 December, and Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy a Senate subcommittee on 2 December, where his $50 billion funding request will be questioned.

      FAA stops all departures across the US

      Just before 4 pm US Eastern time on Friday, the FAA orders a ground stop for all US airports after "communication issues impacting multiple Air Route Traffic Control Centers", which guide planes at high altitude between airports.       Duffy blames old equipment: "It got overloaded"

      At a late-night press conference, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says old switches and routers that carry air traffic data were overloaded and began giving inconsistent data. Mullin says new security patches added to the older system appear to have overwhelmed the routers, and that the outage is not linked to the Microsoft outage a day earlier. Duffy warns there will be more such outages unless the system is upgraded and calls for funding, saying "This is an emergency."

      More than 3,400 Saturday flights cancelled as airlines recover

      FlightAware counts more than 3,400 flights within, into or out of the US cancelled by Saturday morning (6:45 am US Eastern, 5:15 pm IST), as planes and crews are out of place after Friday's stop. Delta has cancelled 629 flights, about 20% of its schedule, and JetBlue 335, about 40%. Delta is letting passengers change Friday and Saturday flights without extra cost; United is waiving change fees for travel between 12 and 20 November. Officials say the system should be working normally in time for Thanksgiving travel later this month.

    7. Saturday, 14 Nov (US daytime; evening IST)2026
      Saturday, 14 Nov (US daytime; evening IST) Travel New since your last visit Stated · US Transportation Secretary, FAA

      Duffy blames contractor L3Harris; FAA finds "zero evidence of anything nefarious"

      At a Saturday briefing, Duffy says the outage began while the contractor L3Harris, which runs the FAA's telecoms network, was switching lines from copper to fibre at a remote FAA site, and a router "started flooding other routers". He says officials are checking "whether we had incompetence" by the contractor or a faulty router, and that "We're going to hold people accountable." FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford says officials found "zero evidence of anything nefarious and zero evidence of anything AI related", and that the review will show whether the router was installed or set up wrongly, or was faulty. The FAA has switched off the problem function on about 60 routers of the same type. L3Harris says it is supporting the FAA's investigation. CBS News, citing senior government sources, says the problem was isolated to old Cisco routers.

    11. Sunday, 15 Nov2026
      Sunday, 15 Nov Travel New since your last visit Reported · FlightAware data; Stated · Duffy, FAA

      Third day of disruption; Duffy expects "fully normal" flights by Monday

      Cancellations continued on Sunday US time, though fewer than on Saturday. Reuters, citing the flight tracker FlightAware, reported more than 2,300 US flights cancelled and about 7,700 delayed, with JetBlue (28% of its flights) and American Airlines (17%) worst hit. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy told ABC's "This Week" that flights should be back to normal on Sunday "but probably fully normal" on Monday. He said there was no evidence of a cyber attack, "but we don't actually have the root cause." The FAA said it had switched off the routers where the fault started, and about 60 similar ones. Duffy is now asking Congress for $50 billion to replace air traffic control equipment, up from $30 billion; President Trump wrote that "Congress must approve." Two Democratic lawmakers said about $11 billion of the $12.5 billion Congress approved last year is still unspent.

      Monday morning: a few hundred cancellations, American hit hardest

      Early on Monday, US time, a few hundred flights had been cancelled, far fewer than over the weekend, per the flight tracker FlightAware. As of 7 am Eastern time (5:30 pm IST), 284 flights within, into or out of the US had been cancelled and 954 delayed, with American Airlines cancelling the most (157), per Fox Business. The FAA has traced the abnormal network traffic to sites in Amarillo, Texas, and El Dorado, Arkansas; investigators have not determined whether faulty equipment, the installation or something else caused it. Staff from the FAA and Transportation Department were due to brief House committees on Monday about the $30 billion funding request, per a statement from two Democratic lawmakers reported by CBS News.

    14. 18–19 Nov (US time)2026
      18–19 Nov (US time) Travel New since your last visit Stated · FAA, Transportation Department

      FAA pauses router installs; cause still unknown and oversight disputed

      The FAA has suspended installation of the router model linked to the failure, Engadget reports, and FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said the agency still does not know what triggered it. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the same upgrade had 'worked well' 68 times before, blamed the contractor L3Harris for being slow to isolate the problem, and asked the FBI to help examine the router in Arkansas, per AP and Engadget. Bedford said: 'I take full responsibility for it.' The router's maker, HPE, says the routers were 'working exactly as designed', per ABC News. Investigators are checking whether the router was set up wrongly or was faulty, AP reports. Bedford said the contractor's engineer was monitored by an FAA technician; the air traffic technicians' union PASS and two sources dispute that, per Scripps News, and an FAA spokeswoman later said the escort 'is not there while the technician is doing work'. L3Harris declined further comment.

    15. 19–20 Nov (US time)2026
      19–20 Nov (US time) Policy New since your last visit Confirmed · President Trump

      White House cyber director removed after a string of outages

      Sean Cairncross, the White House National Cyber Director, has been removed from his post, after the FAA ground stop and other technology outages drew criticism, Fox News and The National Desk report. President Trump did not dispute that he was fired, saying: 'Because I didn't think that person was good enough.' Trump called the outages 'all separate events', and Secretary of State Marco Rubio said officials found 'zero evidence' linking them to each other or to hackers, per Fox News. Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon's chief information officer, is to take over. Cairncross said it was 'an honor to serve'. Trump also urged Congress to provide $50 billion for air traffic control upgrades.

    What this means for you

    Flying to, from or within the US this weekend? Check your flight status with the airline before leaving for the airport. Delta and United are letting affected passengers change flights without a fee. Under US Transportation Department rules, if your flight is cancelled and you do not accept another flight, you are entitled to a refund.
