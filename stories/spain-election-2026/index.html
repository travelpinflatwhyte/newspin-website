    Spain's snap election: from housing protests to the 29 November vote

    Pedro Sánchez called an early election after parliament rejected his housing plans. Polls put the conservative PP ahead, with the far-right Vox third.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Spain votes on 29 November in a general election called early by Socialist (PSOE) prime minister Pedro Sánchez. He dissolved parliament on 5 October after it rejected two of his government's bills to protect renters from eviction, amid large protests over housing costs. Most polls put the conservative People's Party (PP), led by Alberto Núñez Feijóo, first and the far-right Vox, led by Santiago Abascal, third.

    Why it matters

    Spain is the European Union's fourth-largest economy. Some polls project that the PP and Vox together could win a majority, which could bring Vox into national government talks for the first time. Housing costs, corruption cases around the Socialists, and migration are the main campaign issues.

    What’s next

    Votes are counted on the night of 29 November. If no party wins 176 seats, parties must negotiate before parliament votes on a prime minister. About 163,000 overseas ballots are kept apart until Spain's Supreme Court rules on whether they count.

    1. 4–5 Oct2026
      4–5 Oct 2026 Policy New since your last visit Confirmed · Spanish government

      Sánchez dissolves parliament and calls an election for 29 November

      Prime minister Pedro Sánchez said he would hold an extraordinary cabinet meeting to dissolve parliament and call a general election, set for 29 November. The move came after parliament rejected two government bills meant to protect tenants from eviction. Tens of thousands of people had protested across Spain over housing costs. Sánchez's minority government was also under pressure over corruption cases involving the Socialist Party and its handling of a migration crisis in Spain's North African city of Ceuta.

    2. 12 Oct2026
      12 Oct 2026 Law New since your last visit Reported

      Supreme Court keeps about 163,000 overseas voters' rights suspended

      Spain's Supreme Court refused to lift a suspension of voting rights for about 163,000 people who became Spanish citizens under the "grandchildren's law", which lets descendants of people who fled Spain apply for nationality. The court questioned a government instruction that widened who qualifies. Consulates are keeping these voters' ballots in separate boxes, and they will not be counted unless the court later allows it.

    3. 13 Nov2026
      13 Nov 2026 World New since your last visit Reported

      The official campaign opens, with housing, corruption and migration at the centre

      The two-week campaign began at midnight on 13 November. Sánchez opened in Seville, the PP's Alberto Núñez Feijóo near Barcelona, Vox's Santiago Abascal in Huesca and Ada Colau of the left-wing Frente Amplio in Madrid. Vox proposes giving Spaniards priority for housing; the PP has said it would repeal the new renter protections that parliament later passed. Feijóo has not ruled out a coalition with Vox under strict conditions, according to the BBC.

    4. 23 Nov2026
      23 Nov 2026 Data New since your last visit Reported

      Final polls put the PP narrowly ahead and the right-wing bloc above a majority

      The last tracking poll by 40dB for El País and Cadena SER put the PP on 31.8%, the Socialists on 30.9% and Vox on 17.3%. It projected that the PP and Vox together would win 187 seats, above the 176 needed for a majority. The government-run CIS pollster instead put the Socialists first on 34.4%; other pollsters have not shown the Socialists that high. Polls are estimates, and results may differ.

    5. 23–25 Nov2026
      23–25 Nov 2026 World New since your last visit Allegation · PP leader Feijóo

      PP leaders question overseas voting; Sánchez calls the system clean

      In a TV interview on 23 November, Feijóo said "there has been electoral fraud" linked to the grandchildren's law, accusing the government of setting a "trap" by widening who could become a citizen. On 25 November he said he was concerned about the fairness of overseas voting, and Madrid regional leader Isabel Díaz Ayuso said PP figures were worried about fraud. Sánchez said he was not sure the PP and Vox would accept the result if they fell short, and called the voting system "absolutely guaranteed, clean and internationally recognised".

    What this means for you

    Nothing changes for travellers or Spanish residents on election day itself. Policies on housing and migration, the main campaign issues, would change only after a new government is formed and passes new laws.
