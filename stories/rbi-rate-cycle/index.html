    RBI’s interest-rate cycle: from five cuts to its first hike in over three years

    How the Reserve Bank went from cutting rates to raising them in 20 months, and what it means for your loans and the economy.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    On 7 Oct 2026 the RBI raised its key rate to 5.50%, its first increase since February 2023, after cutting it five times in 2025.

    Why it matters

    Inflation picked up sharply through mid-2026. Borrowers with repo-linked floating loans may see higher EMIs at their next reset.

    What’s next

    The next policy meeting is 2–4 Dec 2026. With October inflation at 6.09%, 56 of 61 economists in a Reuters poll and all 14 in a Moneycontrol poll expect a rise to 5.75%.

    13. 20 Nov2026
      20 Nov 2026 Data New since your last visit Reported · Reuters poll

      Reuters poll: most economists expect a December hike to 5.75%

      In a Reuters poll taken from 13 to 20 November, 56 of 61 economists expect the RBI to raise the repo rate by 0.25 percentage points to 5.75% at its 2–4 December meeting. Three expect no change and two expect a bigger rise. Most (34 of 46) expect one more rise, to 6%, in February. Economists cite higher energy costs from the Middle East war and a patchy monsoon, and expect inflation to stay above the RBI's 4% target this year and next. This is a forecast by economists, not an RBI decision.

    What this means for you

    Home and car loans linked to the repo rate may cost more at their next reset, depending on your loan terms. Fixed-rate loans you already have are not affected.
