    Jensen Huang resigns, then returns: how the Nvidia espionage reports unfolded

    A report that Nvidia's founder was targeted by Chinese intelligence led to a board inquiry, his resignation, a share slide, regulator inquiries and, 11 days later, his reinstatement.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO on 23 November, 11 days after he resigned. He left after The New York Times reported that US intelligence found he had a close relationship with a Megaspeed executive they concluded was working for Beijing, and a second relationship, with a senior Nvidia executive, was reported. Since then California's attorney general has subpoenaed Nvidia, Reuters and the Times have reported an SEC inquiry into its accounting, and the US has put Megaspeed and six other companies on its trade blacklist.

    Why it matters

    Nvidia makes the chips behind most of the world's AI and is one of the most valuable companies on earth. The board says senior US officials told it Huang's return poses no national security risk, but it has not named them, and several investigations are still open.

    What’s next

    Huang is due to be questioned behind closed doors by House committees on 1 December, and Nvidia's general counsel on 2 December. Senate Banking Committee chair Tim Scott wants public testimony; a hearing is reported for 3 December. The board's own investigation continues.

    31. 23 Nov 2026 (US time)2026
      23 Nov 2026 (US time) Trade New since your last visit Confirmed · US Commerce Department (Federal Register filing), via reports

      US puts Megaspeed and six other companies on its trade blacklist

      The US Commerce Department added Megaspeed, the Singapore-based company at the centre of the reports about Huang, to its Entity List with six other companies in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand. They include Apex Enterprise Solutions, a Singapore unit of Alibaba. US firms now need a licence to sell them American goods, software or technology, including chips, and applications are normally refused. Commerce says the companies pose a "significant risk" of passing advanced computing items to unauthorised users. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing. Alibaba called the listing of its unit "erroneous" and said it will seek legal remedies. Nvidia says it stopped selling to Megaspeed in June 2025. US prosecutors are running a criminal investigation into Megaspeed.
