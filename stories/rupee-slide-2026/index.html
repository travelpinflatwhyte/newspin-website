    The rupee in 2026: a record slide, then a rebound

    From near 89 to the dollar in January to a record low near 96.90, then a rebound in November. What moved the rupee this year.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    The rupee hit a string of record lows in 2026, bottoming near 96.90 per dollar. It strengthened for most of November. On 25 November it closed at 92.20, per PTI's revised figure: an eight-month high that erased its losses since April. On 26 November it slipped 24 paise to 92.44 (provisional) as oil prices rose and the proposed US weighed.

    Why it matters

    A weaker rupee makes imports like oil, electronics and foreign travel or education more expensive, and adds to inflation.

    What’s next

    Falling oil prices, foreign inflows and RBI dollar sales have helped. A stronger dollar on US rate-rise bets, or a renewed oil spike, would push the other way.

      Rupee crosses 93 and closes at a seven-month high of 92.67

      The rupee closed at 92.67 per dollar on Friday (provisional), 65 paise stronger than Thursday and its strongest level in seven months, PTI reports. On the two days before, PTI reported closes of 93.44 (provisional) on Wednesday and 93.32 on Thursday.

      Forex reserves fall $14.2 billion to a three-month low; rupee on track for its best month in eight years

      India's foreign exchange reserves fell by $14.20 billion to $705.34 billion in the week to 13 November, the lowest in about three months, RBI data released on Friday show, per The Tribune and Moody's via TradingView. Foreign currency assets fell $7.90 billion and gold holdings $6.18 billion. Forex reserves are the dollars, other currencies and gold the RBI holds, partly to steady the rupee. Separately, Business Standard reports the rupee is up 2.46% in November so far, on course for its best monthly gain in more than eight years, helped by nearly $13 billion of foreign investment.

    15. 25 Nov2026
      25 Nov 2026 Markets New since your last visit Market data · PTI, AIR News

      Rupee rises 69 paise to close at 92.16, an eight-month high that erases its losses for the financial year

      The rupee opened at 92.65 per dollar on Wednesday and closed at 92.16 (provisional), up 69 paise from Monday's close of 92.85, PTI reports. PTI calls it the rupee's highest level this year and an eight-month high; it wipes out all the rupee's losses since the financial year began in April. The rupee has gained in five straight sessions since 94.19 on 13 November. Currency markets were shut on Tuesday for Guru Nanak Jayanti. Analysts quoted by PTI credit strong buying in Indian shares, foreign investor inflows, cheaper crude and RBI steps to improve dollar supply; a firm US dollar limited the gains. Foreign investors bought a net ₹5,672.99 crore of Indian shares on Monday, per exchange data cited by PTI. crude was at $91.55 a barrel. A stronger rupee makes imports such as fuel and foreign travel slightly cheaper.

    16. 26 Nov2026
      26 Nov 2026 Markets New since your last visit Market data · PTI

      Rupee slips 24 paise to 92.44 as oil rises and US tariff worries weigh

      The rupee opened at 92.50 per dollar on Thursday and closed at 92.44 (provisional), down 24 paise, PTI reports. It moved between 92.31 and 92.66 during the day. Forex traders blamed higher crude oil prices and worries over the proposed US of up to 25% on Indian goods; foreign investor inflows limited the fall. Rajeev Sharan of Brickwork Ratings said Brent crude climbing back above $95 a barrel and the tariff proposal were the two pressures. Brent was at $93.80 a barrel in futures trade at the time of the report. Foreign investors bought a net ₹4,479.08 of Indian shares on Wednesday, per exchange data. PTI now gives Wednesday's close as 92.20, up 65 paise, not the provisional 92.16 shown earlier.
