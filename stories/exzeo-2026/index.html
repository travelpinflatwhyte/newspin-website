    Exzeo: from HCI's tech arm to a listed insurance-software company

    Home-insurance software maker whose platform is built in India. It listed in New York in 2025; growth has since stalled and the shares trade below the IPO price.

    Disclosure: Newspin's founder previously worked at Exzeo. This timeline follows the same sourcing rules as every other story.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Exzeo makes software that home insurers use to quote, sell and manage policies and handle claims. It began as the in-house technology arm of Florida insurer HCI Group, listed on the New York Stock Exchange in November 2025 at $21 a share, and HCI still owns most of it. Its third-quarter revenue was flat at $55.2 million.

    Why it matters

    Exzeo is testing whether software built for one insurer can be sold to many. It still depends on HCI for substantially all of its revenue. Its platform is developed by its Indian subsidiary, which has an office in Noida.

    What’s next

    Exzeo expects of about $1.34 billion at the end of 2026, lower than its earlier $1.55 billion target. Fourth-quarter results are due in early 2027; no date has been announced.

      3 Mar 2025 Policy New since your last visit Confirmed · HCI Group

      HCI Group splits into insurance and technology units and renames its tech arm Exzeo

      Florida insurer HCI Group said it was reorganising into two operating units: its insurance companies, and its technology business. It renamed TypTap Insurance Group as Exzeo Group. Exzeo began in 2012 as HCI's in-house technology and innovation division, building the software HCI's insurers use to quote, sell and manage home insurance policies. HCI chairman and CEO Paresh Patel said: "Our technology has a proven track record of delivering solid underwriting results."

      HCI switches to a stock market listing for Exzeo

      With its second-quarter results in August, HCI said it would list Exzeo through an initial public offering (IPO) instead of a spin-off, and that it had confidentially submitted draft listing papers to the US Securities and Exchange Commission. Exzeo president Kevin Mitchell said: "We believe the best path is to pursue an initial public offering of Exzeo shares."

      Exzeo prices its IPO at $21 a share, raising $168 million

      Exzeo sold 8 million new shares at $21, the middle of its $20–22 range, raising $168 million, and listed on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker XZO.       Full-year 2025: revenue up 62% to $217 million; flood insurance deal with Tokio Marine

      Exzeo reported 2025 revenue of $217.0 million, up 62% from $133.9 million, and net income from continuing operations of $82.7 million. Managed premium reached $1.39 billion. On the results call, chief financial officer Suela Bulku said the company expected 2026 pre-tax income of $115 million to $125 million. The same day Exzeo announced a partnership with Tokio Marine Highland to offer a home flood insurance product in 42 US states.
