    SpaceX's Starship: every test flight, from first launch to the first ship catch

    How the world's largest rocket went from losing both stages on its first flight in 2023 to being caught by its launch tower after almost a day in space, and what comes next.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Starship is SpaceX's giant two-stage rocket: a Super Heavy booster and the Starship spacecraft on top, both meant to be reused. On Flight 15 the spacecraft was caught by the arms of its launch tower in Texas after about 22.5 hours in space, the first time a rocket's upper stage has been recovered this way. The booster's catch was called off and it came down in the Gulf of Mexico.

    Why it matters

    Reusing both stages, like an aircraft, could make launches much cheaper. Starship is central to SpaceX's next-generation Starlink satellites and to NASA's plan to land astronauts on the Moon.

    What’s next

    SpaceX is expected to try catching both stages on the next flight, and to begin launching from Florida. Mexico's environment agency is checking debris that washed up near the border after Flight 15; SpaceX says it found very little. Mexico wants talks with the US on Starship flights over its airspace. NASA's Moon plans need a demonstration of refuelling in orbit, planned for 2027.

    16. 17–19 Nov2026
      17–19 Nov 2026 Tech New since your last visit Stated · Profepa, SpaceX

      Debris on a Mexican beach; SpaceX says it found very little

      Mexico's federal environment agency, Profepa, said debris began washing up on Playa Bagdad in Tamaulipas, just across the border from Starbase, on the morning of 17 November, after the Flight 15 booster came down in the Gulf of Mexico. Profepa said it would take custody of the material and ask SpaceX to take responsibility for anything traced to its launches. The conservation group Conibio reported plastic, tape and adhesive fragments offshore. On 19 November SpaceX said its teams found very few fragments, that their origin was not confirmed, and that social media claims of widespread damage were false or misleading. It cited independent tests finding no chemical, biological or toxicological risk in Starship's materials and offered to help if Mexican authorities ask. Investigators have not yet said which pieces came from Flight 15.

    17. 21–23 Nov2026
      21–23 Nov 2026 World New since your last visit Stated · Mexican authorities; Allegation · Conibio Global; SpaceX disputes

      Mexico says SpaceX must take responsibility for suspected debris near Matamoros

      Mexican authorities said SpaceX must take responsibility for suspected rocket debris washing up on beaches near Matamoros, across the border from Starbase, per Texas Public Radio and the Texas Tribune. An environmental group, Conibio Global, says it has found at least 1,500 fragments that look like plastic, rubber and adhesive, and believes they came from the Flight 15 booster; it says this still needs confirming. A dead sea turtle is being examined. Mexico's environment agency, Profepa, told people not to touch fragments. SpaceX said on 19 November that its partners found "very little debris" and called claims of widespread damage "outright fabrications or intentionally misleading". No date has been announced for Flight 16.

    18. 26 Nov2026
      26 Nov 2026 World New since your last visit Stated · President Sheinbaum; SpaceX disputes debris link

      Mexico seeks talks with the US on Starship flights over its airspace

      Mexico's President Claudia Sheinbaum said her government will seek, through its embassy, a meeting between US and Mexican airspace authorities to set terms for Starship flights over Mexico, including how often they happen. She said the launch site could be moved "if necessary", and that the suspected debris near Matamoros is a separate issue. Mexico's navy is holding the debris, and its environment agency, Profepa, says it will ask SpaceX to collect it. SpaceX says it is working with Mexican authorities, that partners found "very little debris", and that what was found is "not clearly from SpaceX operations". SpaceX has asked US regulators to allow many more launches a year from Starbase.

    What this means for you

    Starship is how SpaceX plans to launch its next-generation Starlink internet satellites, which are bigger and faster than today's. If both stages can be caught and flown again quickly, the cost of getting things into space could fall sharply, which affects everything from satellite internet to Moon missions.
