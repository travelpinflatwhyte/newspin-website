Why flying costs more: jet fuel, surcharges and new fare rules

Jet fuel has risen four months in a row, airlines have added fuel surcharges, and the government has given itself new powers over ticket prices from January.

In 30 seconds

For everyone

What happened

Jet fuel, also called aviation turbine fuel or ATF, makes up roughly 35–40% of an airline's costs. Since the Strait of Hormuz closed, its price in India has kept climbing, and airlines have added fuel surcharges to tickets. On 2 November the government notified rules that let it cap fares in an "extraordinary situation".

Why it matters

Higher fuel costs mean higher fares and fewer flights. Airlines scheduled 2.6% fewer weekly flights in November than in October 2025, and domestic passenger numbers fell 6.5% year on year in September.

What’s next

The Supreme Court hears a petition on high fares on 16 November. Jet fuel prices are revised on the 1st of each month, and the new fare rules take effect on 1 January 2027.

    New rules let the government cap fares

    The civil aviation ministry notifies Rule 192 of the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026. The government can order limits on fares in an "extraordinary situation", for a set period. Airlines must publish fares and show a full breakup of base fare, taxes, fees and cancellation charges, and the DGCA will monitor fares. The rules apply from 1 January 2027. Some experts quoted by the Free Press Journal say the rules do not define an extraordinary situation.

What this means for you

Fuel surcharges are part of the ticket price, so compare the total fare, not the base fare. From 1 January 2027, airlines must show a full breakup: base fare, taxes, fees, cancellation charges and refund conditions. Booking early and avoiding festival dates is still the main way to pay less.
