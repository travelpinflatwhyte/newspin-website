    China's AI labs: DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi and the open-model race

    Cheap, open models from DeepSeek, Alibaba, Moonshot and Zhipu closed much of the gap with US labs, and sparked accusations of copying.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    DeepSeek's R1 shook the industry in January 2025. In 2026 Chinese labs released a wave of large models, including DeepSeek V4, Moonshot's Kimi K3, Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max and Zhipu's GLM-5.2, often far cheaper than US rivals. In November Alibaba released Qwen4-Max and Zhipu GLM-5.4.

    Why it matters

    Open, cheap models let anyone build AI products without paying US companies, and they sit at the centre of the US–China technology contest.

    What’s next

    Alibaba plans open versions of smaller Qwen4 models, and DeepSeek has confirmed a V4.1 Pro; neither has a date. Reuters reports that US computing limits could delay Zhipu's GLM-5.5 to 2027 and Moonshot's Kimi K4 to around mid-2027.

    10. 9 Nov2026
      9 Nov 2026 AI New since your last visit Released · Meituan

      Meituan opens up LongCat-2.5, a 1.6-trillion-parameter agent model

      Meituan, the Chinese food-delivery company, released the weights of LongCat-2.5 under the MIT licence, so anyone can download and use it. AI/TLDR, citing the model card, says it has 1.6 trillion parameters, of which about 48 billion are used for each word it produces, which keeps running costs down. It can read screenshots and video and is built for long, multi-step coding and agent tasks. Meituan's own test scores show large gains over the earlier LongCat-2.0; these are the company's measurements, not independent ones.

    12. 16 Nov2026
      16 Nov 2026 AI New since your last visit Released · Alibaba

      Alibaba releases Qwen4-Max, its new flagship

      Alibaba makes Qwen4-Max generally available after a preview that began on 11 November. It is a 2.4-trillion-parameter model that can read up to one million tokens at once. Unlike many Chinese models, its weights are closed, so it cannot be downloaded. Alibaba says it beats Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.1 and Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro on some coding tests, and that smaller Qwen4 models will be released openly, with no date. API prices are $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens, per The Next Web.

      17 Nov 2026 (US time) Trade New since your last visit Stated · Nvidia, via Reuters

      Nvidia stops shipping US-approved H200 chips to China

      Nvidia's interim CEO Colette Kress said on the company's earnings call that it is "no longer shipping licensed Hopper data centre products to China", which include the H200 chips Washington had approved for some Chinese buyers, Reuters reported. Nvidia sold $2.4 billion of these chips to China in the quarter to 25 October, and its forecast includes none. She did not say why or whether the halt is permanent. Beijing has also limited imports of the H200 to cut reliance on US technology. For Chinese AI labs, it means less new supply of the US chips used to train and run large models.

    14. 23 Nov2026
      23 Nov 2026 AI New since your last visit Released · Zhipu

      Zhipu releases GLM-5.4, built by running its layers twice

      Zhipu (Z.ai) released GLM-5.4, its new flagship. Instead of adding layers, it runs 28 layers of its GLM-5 base model twice, a "loop" design meant to improve reasoning without more parameters. Zhipu says it matches models of 2 to 3 trillion parameters, from a 750-billion-parameter base; it has not yet published benchmark scores. It adds image understanding and, per TechNode, a one-million-token . It is available in Zhipu's coding plan and chat app. The API and open weights will follow safety checks, with no date.

    15. 23 Nov 2026 (US time)2026
      23 Nov 2026 (US time) Trade New since your last visit Confirmed · US Commerce Department, via reports

      US blacklists an Alibaba unit and Megaspeed over chip diversion

      The US Commerce Department put seven companies on its Entity List, including Megaspeed of Singapore and Apex Enterprise Solutions, Alibaba's Singapore unit. US firms now need a licence, normally refused, to sell them chips and other technology. Commerce says they pose a "significant risk" of passing advanced computing items to unauthorised users. Alibaba called the listing "erroneous", said the unit "has never diverted" restricted chips and plans legal action. Beijing called US claims of a Chinese intelligence operation "groundless speculation".

    16. 26 Nov2026
      26 Nov 2026 Tech New since your last visit Reported · Reuters (anonymous sources)

      Reuters: US chip rules and copying defences are delaying Zhipu's and Moonshot's next models

      Zhipu (Z.AI) planned to release its flagship GLM-5.5, with about 3 trillion parameters (the internal settings a model learns), in November but has not, Reuters reported, citing two unnamed sources. New US limits on renting computing power abroad could push it to early 2027 or later. One source said better defences against 'distillation', training a model on a rival model's answers, are part of the delay. Moonshot's gap between major Kimi models could grow from about six months to a year or more if it loses overseas computing, and Kimi K4 could slip to around mid-2027, per one person. The US rule took effect on 19 November and requires licences for large amounts of computing power from advanced US chips in overseas data centres. Zhipu did not immediately comment to Reuters.
