    Delhi's air in winter 2026: every stage, every spike

    From a clean October day to Diwali smog, Stage III curbs and their lifting. How Delhi's air turned this season, and what the rules mean for you.

    In 30 seconds

    For everyone

    What happened

    Delhi's air turned 'severe' on 13 November, with at 403, then eased into the 'poor' band: 214 at 7 am on 18 November, 289 at 8:30 am on 19 November, 269 at 8 am on 20 November, the season's coldest morning so far, 278 on 21 November, with 25 of 57 stations 'very poor', 237 at 9 am on 22 November, a daily average of 227 on 24 November, 220 on 25 November, 224 on 26 November, 226 on 27 November and 252 at 9 am on 28 November, with six stations 'very poor'. Curbs moved from Stage I on 21 October to Stage III on 6 November; the air commission lifted Stage III on 17 November, leaving Stage I and II in force. Diwali brought the worst festival-day air since 2021.

    Why it matters

    Air in the 'very poor' and 'severe' bands harms everyone's lungs, especially children, older people and anyone with asthma or heart disease. Each stage adds limits on construction, vehicles and burning.

    What’s next

    Saturday morning's AQI was 252 ('poor'), with six stations 'very poor'. The official forecast expected 'very poor' air on 28 and 29 November as winds stay weak. Stage I and II curbs stay. The air commission wants Delhi and nearby states to cut pollution by 15–20% in 2027. Out-of-state vehicles that are not BS-VI are barred from Delhi until 31 January 2027.

      Thursday's AQI 224, 'poor' for a 12th day; 'very poor' forecast for 28–29 November

    What this means for you

    Check the AQI before going out and limit outdoor exercise on 'very poor' and 'severe' days. Stage III is lifted, so Delhi-registered BS-III petrol and BS-IV diesel cars can drive again and Delhi schools are back to in-person classes. If your car is registered outside Delhi and is not BS-VI, it can still be turned back at the border until 31 January.
